A dripping faucet is more than an annoyance. A drip that runs all day wastes water, and a leak under the sink can rot the cabinet floor before you notice. Most faucet faults come from worn internal parts, mineral buildup or a failing supply line.

If a faucet will not shut off or water is spraying under the sink, close the small shutoff valve under the sink and call (813) 471-5608. The line is open 24/7. If the water is not coming from the fixture itself but from hidden pipes, leak detection is the better page to read.

What to try before you call

  1. Close the shutoff valve. Turn the oval handle under the sink clockwise to stop the supply.
  2. Check the aerator. Unscrew the tip, rinse out grit and mineral scale, and test the flow.
  3. Look under the sink. Dry the supply lines and see where the drip comes back.
  4. Tighten what is loose. A loose handle or nut can be snugged gently by hand.
  5. Call if it still leaks. A drip from inside the faucet body usually needs a new cartridge.

Water quality and your fixtures

Minerals in water leave scale on aerators, shower heads and the inside of a cartridge. If your faucets clog or stiffen quickly, scale may be the reason. You can test your tap, or ask your water utility for its latest water quality report. If your water is hard, a softener or filter may help, and our water softener and filtration page explains what to consider.

Repair or replace

Repair makes sense when the faucet is fairly new and only a part has failed. Replacement makes sense when the finish is worn through, the body is pitted or parts are no longer made. If you are changing the faucet for looks or function, we can install what you choose, or help you choose a good fit for your sink holes and counter.

We replace the supply lines when we replace a faucet, since old braided lines can fail with little warning.

Outdoor spigots and hose bibs

Outdoor faucets in Florida see sun, rain and little attention. A leaking hose bib can run under a flower bed unnoticed for months. We replace worn spigots, add vacuum breakers that keep hose water from flowing back into the house and check the pipe behind the wall. If the bib is leaking inside the wall, it becomes a water line repair or leak detection job.

Code and standards

ASSE 1011 is the standard for hose connection vacuum breakers, the device that keeps hose water from flowing back into the house supply. The EPA's WaterSense label also covers bathroom faucets that flow at 1.5 gallons per minute or less, which is useful when you are choosing a replacement.
