Business emergency: first steps

While you wait for the plumber

  • Shut off the water at the main valve if water is flowing.
  • Keep customers and staff away from the affected area.
  • Move inventory, paperwork and electronics out of the water's path.
  • Do not use floor drains or restrooms if the line is backing up.
  • Photograph the damage and note the time, for your insurance company.

Kitchens and grease

Restaurants and food service businesses place the heaviest demand on drain lines. Grease, starch and food solids build up in kitchen lines, and the result is slow sinks, odors and sudden shutdowns. Regular line cleaning and a grease trap that is serviced on schedule avoid most emergencies.

Hydro jetting is often the right tool for a grease-coated commercial line. A grease trap itself is usually pumped by a separate service, and we tell you if that is the problem.

Backflow assemblies and testing

Many commercial properties have backflow prevention assemblies on the water supply or irrigation. Local rules govern testing and who may perform it, and the water utility enforces them. Ask your water provider about the requirements for your property. If an assembly is leaking or damaged, we can assess it and tell you whether it needs repair or a certified tester.

Storm drains and outdoor lines

Parking lots and loading areas have storm drains, and heavy Florida rain can overwhelm them. A blocked storm drain floods a lot or a walkway. We can clear a blocked line and check it with a camera. Drainage design and larger civil work are not part of what we do, and we say so up front.

Scope and honesty

We focus on emergency repair and maintenance for small and mid-size commercial properties. Large new construction, specialty process piping and fire sprinkler systems are different trades, and we will tell you when a job is outside our scope instead of overpromising.

Code and standards

Commercial plumbing falls under the Florida Building Code, Plumbing. The IPC section on protection of the potable water supply covers backflow prevention, and grease interceptors and backflow assemblies are also subject to local utility rules. Ask your water provider and the building department what applies to your property.
