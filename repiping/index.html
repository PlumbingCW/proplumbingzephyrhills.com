Repiping replaces the water supply lines in a house with new pipe. It is a bigger job than a repair, and it is not a routine choice. You consider it when pipes keep failing, when the material is a known problem or when water quality and pressure have dropped because the pipe is corroded inside.

If a pipe is leaking right now, close the main valve and call (813) 471-5608. The line is open 24/7. A repair first, and a repipe plan after, is often the sensible order. Our burst pipe repair page covers the first step.

Signs it is time to consider repiping

When replacement beats patching

  • Two or more leaks in the same system within a year or two.
  • Polybutylene pipe, common in homes built from roughly 1978 to 1995.
  • Galvanized steel pipe that is rusted or restricting flow.
  • Brown or rusty water, or a metallic taste, from corrosion inside the pipe.
  • Low pressure that persists after fixtures and the regulator are checked.
  • Pinholes in copper pipe, often with green staining.

PEX is the usual choice for a whole-house repipe because it bends around corners, takes fewer cuts into the walls and does not corrode. Some owners still prefer copper for its record. We explain the price difference and let you decide.

How a repipe is done

  1. Inspect and plan. We check the existing pipe, find the main, the heater and every fixture, and plan the new routes.
  2. Protect the house. We cover floors and furniture and open small access holes.
  3. Run new lines. New pipe goes in through the attic, walls or crawlspace, to every fixture.
  4. Connect and test. We join the new system to the main, test it under pressure and check for leaks.
  5. Patch and clean. Access holes are patched. Texture, paint and finish repairs may be a separate trade.

Slab homes and manufactured homes

Slab-on-grade homes often have water lines running under the floor. Repiping can reroute them overhead through the attic, which avoids breaking the slab. That is the same approach covered under slab leak repair. Manufactured homes have their own supply layout, often below the floor in the belly. Tell us the home type so we can plan accordingly.

What a repipe means for daily life

A whole-house repipe usually takes a few days, depending on the size. Water is off for part of each day. Plan for a mess in the walls and some noise. We try to leave you with water at night when we can. Ask for a schedule before work starts, so you can plan around it, and check whether your area requires a permit.

Expect drywall patches and some paint touch-up. If you have wallpaper or tile where we need access, tell us beforehand.

What affects the price

Cost factors on a repipe

  • House size, number of bathrooms and fixtures.
  • PEX or copper, and the number of fixtures to connect.
  • Slab, one-story or two-story layout and the attic access.
  • Drywall, texture and paint repair after the job.
  • Permits and inspections, which depend on the address.

You get a clear written price before work starts. Insurance does not usually pay for replacing old pipe, though it may cover resulting damage from a sudden leak.

Code and standards

PEX tubing is made to ASTM F876, and drinking water components are certified to NSF/ANSI 61. The IPC sets the rules for water pipe sizing and pressure, and a repipe is permitted work in many jurisdictions, so ask the building department for your address before work begins.
