Water treatment is only worth buying if it solves a real problem, and the only way to know is to test. Spots on dishes, white scale on a showerhead or a smell from the tap are clues, but they do not tell you what is in the water. A test does. We install softeners and filters that match the result.

Call (813) 471-5608 to discuss an installation. This is not usually an emergency service. If water is leaking from an existing treatment unit, shut off its bypass or the main valve and ask for the emergency line, which is open 24/7. Related work includes faucet repair for scaled fixtures.

Test your tap first

  1. Ask your water provider. Your utility publishes a water quality report. Your bill tells you whether that is the City of Zephyrhills or Pasco County Utilities.
  2. Test the tap. A home test kit or a lab test shows hardness, chlorine and other items.
  3. Match the problem. Hardness calls for a softener. Chlorine taste calls for carbon. Sediment calls for a sediment filter.
  4. Choose the size. The number of people, bathrooms and the hardness set the capacity.
  5. Call us with the result. We plan the installation around what the test shows.

A softener does not clean water. It removes hardness minerals, and it does not remove bacteria or most chemicals. Filters target specific contaminants, so the filter type has to match the test result. If a seller recommends a system without a test, ask what problem it is solving.

What installation involves

A whole-house softener goes on the main water line after the pressure regulator and before the water heater. It needs a drain for the regeneration cycle, an electrical outlet and a bypass valve so you can isolate it. We also check your home's water pressure and the condition of the pipe before we connect it.

In a home with polybutylene or heavily corroded pipe, we flag that first, since new equipment will not fix an old pipe problem.

Water heaters and scale

Hard water leaves scale in water heaters and reduces efficiency over time. That is one reason people look into softening. Regular tank flushing also helps. See water heater repair if your heater is already noisy or slow to heat.

Homes on private wells

Some properties outside city service rely on a private well. Well water can have iron, sulfur or bacteria, and each needs a specific approach. A lab test is the right first step, because the answer for a well is not the same as for city water. Tell us on the call if you have a well.

What affects the price

Cost factors on a treatment installation

  • The type and capacity of the system.
  • The test results and the number of treatment stages.
  • Plumbing and drain changes needed at the install location.
  • Electrical outlet and bypass requirements.
  • Salt, filter cartridges and upkeep over time.

You get a clear price before work starts. We do not promise a result a test does not support, and we do not recommend a treatment system you do not need.

Code and standards

Water treatment equipment is tested against published standards. NSF/ANSI 44 covers cation exchange softeners, Standard 42 covers aesthetic items like chlorine taste, Standard 53 covers health-related contaminants and Standard 58 covers reverse osmosis systems. Look for the standard that matches the problem on your test.

FAQ

Questions about water softener and filtration in Zephyrhills

How do I know if I need a water softener?

Test your tap or read your utility's water quality report. Scale on fixtures and spotty dishes suggest hardness, but a test confirms it.

Does a softener make water safe to drink?

No. A softener removes hardness minerals. It is not a purifier. Filters target other items.

Do you install under-sink filters?

Yes. They treat one tap and are a simple install in most kitchens.

Can you service a softener I already own?

Often yes, depending on the model. Call and tell us the brand and what is wrong.

What if my home has a well?

Start with a lab test. Well water problems differ from city water, and the treatment should match the test.
