A tankless water heater heats water as it flows, so there is no tank to run out. That convenience comes with design demands. A gas unit needs a gas line sized for its full burner load and a proper vent.

An electric unit can pull a very large load, and the published specs for a popular 18 kW model list about 75 amps at 240 volts. Check the panel before you buy.

Zephyrhills Pro Plumbing installs and services tankless heaters across Pasco County. For a tank swap, start with water heater installation. If your tankless unit shows an error code or runs cold, call (813) 471-5608 and have the model number handy.

Descaling and flushing

Minerals in the water build scale inside the heat exchanger. Scale narrows the passages and makes the unit overheat, throw error codes or deliver lukewarm water. A flush runs a descaling solution through the exchanger and clears the buildup. How often you need it depends on your water and your usage, so ask the dispatcher what the manufacturer recommends for your model.

Florida groundwater often carries dissolved minerals from the limestone aquifer, and a scale-prone household may need a flush more often. We do not quote a hardness figure for your address because we have not seen your utility's report. Ask your water provider for its annual water quality report, or use a test strip. A water softener can reduce scale load on any heater.

Tankless service steps

  1. Read the error code. We pull the display code and the fault history so we know whether the cause is scale, flow, ignition or venting.
  2. Check the supply. Gas pressure or electrical load is measured, because an undersized supply starves the unit.
  3. Flush the exchanger. A pump circulates descaling solution through the heat exchanger and then rinses it.
  4. Clean the filters. Inlet screens are cleared of grit and debris.
  5. Test output. We run hot water at several fixtures and confirm the temperature rise.

Is tankless right for your Zephyrhills home?

Tankless fits best when you have a gas line with spare capacity, a venting path to the outdoors and a household that uses hot water in long stretches. It can be a poor fit in a small manufactured home where the electrical panel is limited or where venting is hard to route. We will tell you honestly when a tank is the better fit.

Zephyrhills has a high share of older residents, with a median age of 48.8 in the 2020 census. Many households run hot water in short bursts all day and stretch the benefit of a tank. If you have a large family or guests who visit, a tankless heater can handle the peak demand better.

Code and standards

Tankless units draw more power or gas than a tank. The NEC governs wire and breaker sizing for electric models, and the fuel gas code governs gas pipe sizing and venting. The manufacturer's manual sets the minimums, and an installer should follow it so the warranty stays valid.

FAQ

Questions about tankless water heater in Zephyrhills

How often should a tankless heater be flushed?

It depends on your water and your usage. Follow the manufacturer's schedule, and ask us if your unit throws scale-related codes.

Can you service the brand I already have?

Most major brands, yes. Give us the model number when you call.

Will a tankless unit work in my manufactured home?

It can, depending on the electrical panel and the venting path. We check both before quoting.

Does the water temperature setting matter?

Yes. A common default is 120 degrees Fahrenheit. A higher setting raises the scald risk and the scale rate. Our plumbers set it to what the household needs.
