Outdoor Water Faucet Repair: A Zephyrhills, FL Homeowner Guide

Outdoor faucets leak at the handle, at the spout and behind the wall. The first two are usually easy fixes. The third needs a plumber, and ignoring it costs real water.

Call (813) 471-5608

By the Zephyrhills Pro Plumbing team. Published November 13, 2026. Updated November 13, 2026.

An outdoor faucet, also called a hose bib or spigot, is a simple valve on the outside of your house. It has a handle, a stem, a packing seal and a spout. Most leaks come from one of those parts. Some come from the pipe behind the wall, and those are the ones that cost you the most.

If water is spraying or pooling near the foundation, close the main valve and call (813) 471-5608. For a drip you can live with for a day, the steps below help you work out what is wrong. Our faucet repair page covers indoor faucets and the outdoor ones too.

Where is the leak coming from?

Leak location and what it means

Where the water showsLikely causeTypical fix
Under the handle when the faucet is onA worn packing nut or packing washerTighten the nut slightly or replace the packing
From the spout when the faucet is offA worn washer or seat inside the valveReplace the washer or the stem
At the threads where the hose connectsA bad hose washer or cracked spoutReplace the washer or the faucet
On the wall or in the wall under the faucetA leak in the pipe behind the faucet or a failed jointCall a plumber to open and repair the pipe
Water running from the faucet after a freezeA split inside the pipe or stemReplace the faucet and check the pipe

Fixes you can try

  1. Turn off the water. Close the shutoff inside the house if there is one, or the main valve.
  2. Tighten the packing nut. A quarter turn on the nut behind the handle often stops a handle leak.
  3. Replace the washer. Remove the handle and stem, swap the rubber washer and put it back together.
  4. Replace the hose washer. A cheap flat washer inside the hose coupling stops leaks at the hose.
  5. Test it. Turn the water on slowly, check with the hose on and off and look inside the wall's cabinet or crawlspace if there is access.

If the stem is corroded, the faucet is loose in the wall or water leaks from several spots, replacing the whole faucet is faster than repairing it.

Vacuum breakers and backflow

A hose bib vacuum breaker is a small fitting that stops dirty water from flowing back into your house supply if pressure drops. Many hose bibs have one built in, and older ones may not. If a hose is left attached to a sprayer, a pool or a bucket of chemicals, backflow can pull that water into the pipes.

Adding a screw-on vacuum breaker is an inexpensive precaution, and a plumber can fit a new faucet that has one built in.

The leak you cannot see

A faucet that drips at the spout wastes water. A faucet that leaks inside the wall can wet insulation, rot framing and feed mold long before you see it. Signs include a damp spot on the interior wall, a musty smell, a spinning meter with the house shut off and a green patch of lawn near the foundation.

Our leak detection page explains how we trace a leak without opening everything.

Pull the faucet handle off and look. If the pipe at the back of the faucet is wet when the faucet is closed, the problem is behind the valve, and the repair belongs to a plumber.

When to replace the whole faucet

Replace the faucet if the body is cracked, the stem is seized or the threads are stripped. A frost-proof faucet with a long stem is another option. It puts the shutoff inside the heated part of the house, which is more than most Florida homes need, but it is available and fine to use.

If you are replacing a faucet, replace the vacuum breaker and check the supply pipe behind it. A shutoff valve inside the house, if you have one, makes future repairs easier. If you do not, a plumber can add one.

Signs the faucet is leaking into the wall

A leak behind an outdoor faucet is easy to miss because the water drains into the wall cavity or the soil. Look for stained siding or a soft patch of paint under the faucet. Inside the house, check the wall behind the faucet for discoloration, and check any cabinet or garage wall on the other side. A flashlight and a moisture meter make this easier.

Compare your water bill with the same month last year. A sudden jump with no change in how you water the lawn is a cue to run the meter test described in our leak detection guide.

Finally, if the faucet is old, check how it is mounted. A faucet that wiggles in the wall can crack the pipe behind it over time. Securing it with a proper mounting plate prevents the next leak.

Code and standards

ASSE 1011 is the standard for hose connection vacuum breakers, which stop hose water from flowing back into the house supply. Look for that marking when you replace a hose bib or add a screw-on breaker.

A local note on Florida weather and irrigation

Florida homes see strong sun, heavy rain and the occasional hard freeze, and the freeze is the one that surprises people. Freeze watches and warnings reached Pasco County in January 2026, and an exposed hose bib is one of the first places a pipe can split. Disconnect hoses and cover exposed faucets when a freeze is forecast.

Watering rules also matter. The Southwest Florida Water Management District sets irrigation limits for the area, and a leaking outdoor faucet can run on days when you are not allowed to water. Check current district rules for your address.

FAQ

Outdoor faucet questions from Zephyrhills homeowners

Why does my outdoor faucet drip only when the hose is attached?

The hose washer or the threads are usually worn. A new washer is a small fix.

Can I fix a leaking faucet handle myself?

Often yes, with a wrench and a packing nut adjustment. If it keeps leaking after that, call a plumber.

How do I know if the leak is inside the wall?

Look for wet insulation, a damp interior wall or a meter that moves with the house off. A plumber can confirm it.

Should I cover outdoor faucets in Zephyrhills?

During a freeze warning, yes. Disconnect hoses and cover the faucet or the pipe.
