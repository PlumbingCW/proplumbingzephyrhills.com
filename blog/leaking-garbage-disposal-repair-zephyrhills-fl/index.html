Leaking Garbage Disposal Repair in Zephyrhills, FL

A disposal can leak from the top, the side or the bottom. Where the water shows up tells you whether you need a quick fix or a new unit.

Call (813) 471-5608

By the Zephyrhills Pro Plumbing team. Published November 13, 2026. Updated November 13, 2026.

A leaking garbage disposal usually makes itself known as a damp cabinet, a puddle under the sink or a musty smell. The leak is rarely a mystery once you find where the water starts. The top, the sides and the bottom each point to a different cause, and only one of them means the disposal is finished.

If water is pooling, shut off the disposal and its breaker, then call (813) 471-5608. Our garbage disposal repair crews are on call 24/7. Do not put your hand in the disposal, even when it is off.

Find the leak first

  1. Clear the cabinet. Empty the cabinet under the sink and lay down a dry towel or paper.
  2. Dry everything. Wipe the disposal, the drain pipe and the dishwasher hose.
  3. Run water and watch. Fill the sink, let it drain and see where the first drip appears.
  4. Run the dishwasher. Some leaks only show when the dishwasher drains through the disposal.
  5. Mark the spot. Tell the plumber exactly where the water started.

Where it leaks and what that means

Leak locationLikely causeFix
Top, around the sink flangeWorn flange gasket or loose mounting ringReseat the flange and replace the gasket
Side, at the dishwasher hoseLoose clamp or a cracked hoseTighten the clamp or replace the hose
Side, at the drain pipeLoose connection or a worn gasketTighten or replace the fitting

Bottom, near the reset buttonSeal failure in the motor housingReplace the disposal

What you can fix

Top and side leaks are often fixable. Tightening a clamp or nut can stop a drip. Replacing the flange gasket takes longer, since you have to drop the disposal off its mounting ring and re-seat the sink flange with plumber's putty or the gasket supplied. It is a manageable job with the right tools, but a heavy disposal is awkward to hold up while you work.

A bottom leak is different. The housing and seals inside cannot be repaired. Water inside the housing damages the motor, and tightening anything will not help. Replace the unit and check the drain connections while you are there.

Repair or replace?

Replacement is the better choice when the disposal leaks from the housing, when it is more than about ten years old or when it has failed more than once. A new unit also lets you choose a quieter or more powerful model. If the leak is only at a loose connection, tightening is cheaper than replacing.

Before you spend money, check the sink and the drain. A clogged drain downstream can push water up and out of a loose joint and look like a disposal failure. Our drain cleaning page covers that situation.

Protect the cabinet

Water in a cabinet can swell the particleboard base, ruin stored goods and grow mold. Dry the cabinet, run a fan and check the floor of the cabinet with a screwdriver tip. If the board is soft, replace it after the repair. Insurance often covers sudden damage and not wear, so photograph what you find.

Habits that keep a disposal sealed

Everyday care

  • Run cold water while using it and for several seconds afterward.
  • Keep grease, coffee grounds, eggshells in large amounts and fibrous scraps out.
  • Never pour chemical drain cleaners into a disposal.
  • Clean it with ice and citrus peel now and then, not harsh chemicals.
  • Check the cabinet for dampness every few months.

What a replacement visit looks like

A replacement starts with cutting power and removing the old unit from its mounting ring. The plumber checks the sink flange, the drain pipe and the dishwasher connection, then fits a new unit and tests it under running water. If your kitchen has no outlet or switch for the disposal, an electrician may need to add one.

Ask the plumber about the knockout plug on a new unit. If the dishwasher drains through the disposal, the plug must be removed or the dishwasher will back up. Skipping that step is a common mistake and causes a leak that looks like a failed disposal.

Code and standards

The IPC covers how a disposal and dishwasher drain connect and requires backflow protection, and the National Electrical Code (NEC) covers the wiring for the unit. A plumber can tell you what a repair or replacement has to satisfy.

A local note on Zephyrhills kitchens

Many Zephyrhills homes, including the manufactured homes common in the city, have compact sink cabinets. A small leak in a small cabinet reaches the floor fast. Check for dampness often, especially in homes that sit empty for part of the year.

FAQ

Leaking disposal questions from Zephyrhills homeowners

Can I tighten the bolts and stop a disposal leak?

For a loose mounting ring or clamp, yes. Do not overtighten, since that can crack the housing or the flange.

Why is my disposal leaking from the bottom?

The housing or an internal seal has failed. A bottom leak means the unit needs replacing.

Can a leaking disposal damage my cabinet?

Yes. Water can swell wood, grow mold and rust metal. Dry the cabinet and check the base.

How long does a new disposal take to install?

A swap is often done in one visit, depending on the electrical and drain connections.
