How to Replace a Water Heater Heating Element: What Zephyrhills, FL Homes Need to Know

A failed element is one of the cheapest water heater repairs, and one of the easiest to get wrong. Here is how the job works, what to test first and where a homeowner should stop.

Call (813) 471-5608

By the Zephyrhills Pro Plumbing team. Published November 13, 2026. Updated November 13, 2026.

An electric tank water heater has two heating elements, one near the top and one near the bottom. When one fails, you may get lukewarm water, hot water that runs out fast or no hot water at all.

Replacing a heating element is a real repair that many homeowners can do. It also involves 240-volt power and a full tank of water, so the safe path starts with knowing when to stop.

Signs an element has failed

What a bad element looks like

  • Water is lukewarm, not hot, even after a long recovery time.
  • Hot water runs out much faster than it used to.
  • No hot water at all, which can mean both elements or the thermostat.
  • The breaker trips when the heater tries to heat.
  • A higher electric bill with no change in how much hot water you use.

Several faults copy these symptoms. A tripped high-limit reset button, a failed thermostat, a bad breaker or a dip tube that lets cold water mix at the top can all behave like a dead element. That is why testing matters before buying parts.

Test before you replace

  1. Cut the power at the breaker. Switch the heater's double-pole breaker off, then confirm with a non-contact tester at the heater that the wires are dead.
  2. Remove the access panels. Take off the metal cover and fold back the insulation on both elements.
  3. Check the thermostat and reset. Press the red reset button on the upper thermostat. A heater that was tripped may recover.
  4. Test the element with a multimeter. Disconnect one wire from the element and set the meter to ohms. A good element shows a low but real resistance. An open reading means the element is burned out.
  5. Check for a short to ground. Touch one probe to a terminal and the other to the metal tank. Any reading means the element has failed.

If both elements test good, stop. The problem is probably the thermostat or the tank itself, and a plumber should look at it.

How the replacement works

The job is simple in principle. You turn off the power and the cold water supply, drain the tank below the element, unscrew the old element with an element wrench, fit a new gasket and screw in a matching element. Then you refill the tank before power goes back on. Powering a dry element burns it out within seconds.

Dry-fire danger and other mistakes

The most common mistake is turning the breaker on before the tank is full. Open a hot tap in the house and wait for a steady flow of water before you restore power. The second is forgetting to drain the tank enough, which sends water into the wiring box when you pull the element.

The third is reusing the old gasket. A new gasket is cheap and prevents a leak that looks like a failed tank.

Work with the power off and test it again before touching a wire. If the wiring looks scorched, brittle or melted, that is an electrical problem. Call an electrician, and leave the heater off until it is checked.

When replacement is not worth it

An element costs little. The heater around it may not be worth it. If the tank is more than ten years old, has rust at the base or shows moisture around the lower element, the shell may be near the end of its life. Replacing a part in a tank about to leak is a short-term fix. Our water heater replacement page explains how to weigh that decision.

Sediment matters too. A thick layer of scale on the lower element shortens its life and makes a rumbling sound. Flushing the tank each year helps. If you have never flushed it, ask a plumber to look, because old sediment can clog the drain valve.

When to call a plumber

Call when the tank is leaking, when you smell burning, when breakers keep tripping, when the access panel shows water or when you are not comfortable working on 240-volt power. A plumber can test the element, the thermostats and the tank in one visit and tell you honestly which fix is worth paying for.

Tools and parts checklist

Gather these before you start

  • A multimeter that reads ohms, and a non-contact voltage tester.
  • A water heater element wrench that fits the element's socket.
  • A replacement element with the same voltage, wattage and mounting style.
  • A new gasket, since the old one should not be reused.
  • A garden hose to drain the tank, and a bucket and towels for the spill.
  • Insulation tape or a replacement panel cover if the old one is damaged.

Photograph the wiring before you disconnect anything. Two wires attach to each element, and a clear picture makes reassembly simple. Check the rating plate at the same time and write down the model number. The plate is also where you confirm wattage and voltage.

Code and standards

Electrical work on a water heater follows the National Electrical Code (NEC), and the manufacturer's instructions set the element size and wiring. Treat the rating plate on the heater as the final word on voltage and wattage.

A local note on power and permits

Florida allows homeowners to do some work on their own homes, but permit and inspection rules still apply to many plumbing and electrical jobs. A simple element swap is repair work, and a full heater replacement usually is not. Ask the City of Zephyrhills building department, or Pasco County for an address outside city limits, which category your job falls in.

Garages and closets in Florida homes can get hot, which is hard on thermostats and wiring. If the heater sits in an attic or garage, check the access and the clearance before you start.

FAQ

Heating element questions from Zephyrhills homeowners

How long does a heating element last in Zephyrhills?

There is no fixed number. Elements last longer in a clean tank and fail sooner under heavy scale. If yours is more than a few years old and the tank has never been flushed, scale is a likely cause.

Do I need to drain the whole tank?

You need to drain it below the level of the element you are changing. For the lower element, that means most of the tank. For the upper, you can drain less.

Can a heating element trip the breaker?

Yes. A shorted element can trip the breaker as soon as it energizes. Do not keep resetting it. Test the element first.

Is it safe to replace an element on a heater in a manufactured home?

It is the same work, but access can be tight and the wiring may be older. If you see damaged wiring, stop and call a professional.
