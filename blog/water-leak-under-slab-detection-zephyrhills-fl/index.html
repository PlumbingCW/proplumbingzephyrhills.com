Water Leak Under Slab Detection: A Zephyrhills, FL Homeowner Guide

A leak under a concrete slab is hidden, but it is not invisible. The signs show up in the meter, the floor and the sound of the house.

Call (813) 471-5608

By the Zephyrhills Pro Plumbing team. Published November 13, 2026. Updated November 13, 2026.

Most Florida homes sit on concrete slabs, and some water lines run beneath or within them. When one leaks, the water seeps into the soil or rises through the concrete. Detection is about confirming the leak, narrowing the location and avoiding more demolition than needed. Our slab leak repair page covers the repair itself.

If you hear water running with all taps off, feel a hot spot on the floor or see your meter moving, close the main valve and call (813) 471-5608. The line is open 24/7. The faster you act, the less damage the foundation and flooring suffer.

Signs of a slab leak

What homeowners notice

  • A water meter that moves with every fixture off.
  • A warm or hot spot on the floor, often from a hot water line.
  • The sound of running water when no one is using any.
  • A sudden rise in the water bill.
  • Damp carpet, bubbling flooring or cracked tile.
  • A musty smell or mildew near baseboards.
  • Low water pressure throughout the house.

How detection works

  1. Confirm with the meter. A meter test shows whether water is leaving the property.
  2. Isolate the line. A pressure test on hot, cold and irrigation lines shows which is losing pressure.
  3. Listen. Acoustic equipment picks up the sound of escaping water through the floor.
  4. Image. A thermal camera shows warm lines under the floor and wet areas.
  5. Mark the spot. The plumber marks the leak area so any opening is small.

What to do while you wait

Close the main water valve to stop the loss, and open a faucet to relieve pressure. If you hear water or see a hot spot, turn off the water heater to avoid damage. Move furniture off wet floors, photograph the damage and note the time. Keep power off near standing water.

Repair paths once it is found

A found slab leak can be fixed by opening the slab at the spot, rerouting the line through the attic or walls, or repiping the house. The choice depends on the pipe's condition and the number of leaks. For a house with repeated failures, see repiping to weigh the options.

Why slab leaks happen

Copper corrodes, fittings fail, and pipes rub against concrete as the slab moves slightly. Poor bedding or a pipe laid against a sharp rock can wear through. Water chemistry matters too, since aggressive water attacks copper from inside. A plumber can explain which applies to your house after the repair.

Slab leaks are not always supply lines. A drain leak under the slab can also soften soil and cause settlement. A camera inspection of the drain, covered on our sewer camera inspection page, rules that out.

Insurance and documentation

Slab leaks often involve an insurance claim, so keep records from the first sign. Photograph the meter, the damaged floor and any stains. Save the plumber's notes on the leak's location and the method used to find it. Many policies cover the sudden damage a leak causes but not the repair of the pipe itself, so call your insurer early and ask what they require before demolition begins.

If you are buying a home and the inspector suspects a slab leak, ask for a detection test before closing. A leak found early gives you room to negotiate. One found after closing becomes your repair.

Ask the plumber to mark the leak position on the floor and photograph it before any opening is made. That record helps a flooring or insurance contractor later, and it makes sure the repair crew opens the slab in the right place and no larger than needed.

Code and standards

The IPC calls for a pressure-reducing valve where static water pressure exceeds 80 psi. High pressure speeds up wear in pipes and fittings, so ask the plumber to check yours after a slab leak is found.

A local note on Florida soil

Pasco County sits in the limestone region of west-central Florida, where sinkholes are a known hazard. In late summer 2019, 76 ground depressions opened in a Hudson neighborhood in Pasco County, and state geologists tied them to cave systems roughly 15 to 150 feet underground. Most slab leaks have nothing to do with that, but a leak that keeps washing out soil under a slab is a reason to act quickly.

If you see new cracks in walls or floors, doors that stick or a visible dip in the yard, mention it to the plumber and consider a call to a geotechnical professional. Do not assume a leak is the only cause.

FAQ

Slab leak detection questions from Zephyrhills homeowners

Can a slab leak be found without breaking the floor?

Usually the location can be narrowed with listening gear and thermal imaging before any opening is made.

Is a hot spot on the floor always a slab leak?

It can also come from a radiant source or a heater duct. A plumber can confirm with a pressure test.

Will insurance cover slab leak detection?

Policies differ. Many cover resulting damage and some cover the cost of access. Ask your insurer.

How fast should I act?

Right away. A running leak wastes water and can undermine the soil.
