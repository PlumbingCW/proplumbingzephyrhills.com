How to Read Your Water Meter to Find a Leak: A Zephyrhills, FL Guide

Your water meter is the cheapest leak detector you own. A five-minute test tells you whether water is leaving the house when nothing should be running.

Call (813) 471-5608

By the Zephyrhills Pro Plumbing team. Published November 13, 2026. Updated November 13, 2026.

A water meter measures every gallon that enters your property. If water is moving through it when every fixture is off, water is going somewhere. That might be a toilet, an irrigation zone, a pipe in a wall or a service line under the yard. The meter does not tell you where, but it tells you whether you have a leak, and that is where every search starts.

If the test shows a leak, call (813) 471-5608. Our leak detection crews trace it with listening gear and thermal imaging, and the line is open 24/7 for leaks that are actively flooding a home.

Find your meter

Most meters sit in a ground-level box near the street or the property line, often with a lid marked water. Lift the lid with a screwdriver, and look for pests before you reach in. Wipe the glass or plastic face clean.

The meter has a register that counts like an odometer, and many also have a small triangle or wheel that spins with even a slow flow. That is the low-flow indicator.

The five-minute test

  1. Stop all water use. Close faucets, stop the washer and dishwasher, turn off the ice maker and pause irrigation.
  2. Note the reading. Write down the numbers on the register and look at the low-flow indicator.
  3. Wait fifteen to twenty minutes. Do not use any water during that time.
  4. Read it again. If the numbers moved or the indicator is turning, water is leaving the property.
  5. Close the house valve and repeat. If the meter stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is between the meter and the house.

Check the toilets first

A leaking toilet flapper is the most common hidden leak in a house. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank, wait fifteen minutes and do not flush. If color shows in the bowl, the flapper is leaking. It is a small, cheap part. See our toilet repair page for what else can cause running water.

Other places water hides

Common hidden leak sources

  • Irrigation zones with a broken head or stuck valve.
  • A hose bib or outdoor fitting.
  • A water heater relief valve or its discharge pipe.
  • A pipe in a wall, ceiling or slab.
  • A pool fill line or an automatic fill valve.
  • A service line between the meter and the house.

Reading the numbers

Water utilities bill in gallons, hundreds of gallons or cubic feet depending on the system. The label on the face or your bill shows the unit. If you measure the difference between two readings and know the unit, you can estimate how much a leak loses in a day. A small leak adds up. Even a slow drip loses many gallons over a month.

What to do with the result

Write down your readings, the times and what you did. Photograph the meter face. If you plan to ask the water utility for a bill adjustment, those notes help. If the leak is in the house, shut off the supply at the main valve until a plumber arrives, especially if you are going to be away.

A simple log you can keep

Details to write down

  • The date and time of each reading.
  • The register number and the unit on the meter face.
  • Whether the low-flow indicator was turning.
  • Which fixtures and appliances were off during the test.
  • Whether the irrigation system was paused.

Two readings an hour apart on a quiet afternoon are enough to prove a leak to a utility or to your insurer. If the leak is intermittent, repeat the test at different times, including overnight. Some leaks only run when a timer or a valve opens.

Code and standards

According to the EPA's WaterSense program, household leaks can waste nearly 10,000 gallons of water a year in an average home. The meter test in this guide is the fastest way to learn whether yours is one of them.

FAQ

Water meter questions from Zephyrhills homeowners

Can the utility read my meter for me?

Many utilities can check a meter at your request. Call the number on your bill and ask.

My meter moves a little with everything off. Is that a leak?

Yes. Even slow movement suggests water is escaping. A toilet is the usual culprit.

Who owns the pipe between the meter and my house?

Usually the homeowner. Confirm with your water provider.

How long does it take to find a hidden leak?

It depends on the location. A meter test narrows it to a zone, and detection tools pinpoint the spot.
