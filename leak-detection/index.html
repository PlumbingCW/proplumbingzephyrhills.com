Leak detection in Zephyrhills begins with a symptom, not a pipe. Most people call because the water bill climbed, the meter keeps moving with every tap closed, or a ceiling stain has started to spread. Our job is to confirm that a leak exists, narrow down where it is, and tell you what it will take to fix.

If water is actively pouring, shut off the main valve and call (813) 471-5608. The line is open 24/7. For a leak inside a concrete floor, see slab leak repair. For a pipe that has already split, go to burst pipe repair. This page is for the leaks you cannot see yet.

Meter still running or a high water bill

The fastest test is free. Shut off every fixture, make sure no one is running the dishwasher, washer or ice maker, and read the water meter. Most meters have a small low-flow indicator that spins or flickers when even a slow leak is present. Wait twenty minutes with everything off and read it again. Any movement means water is leaving the house.

  1. Turn everything off. Close every faucet and stop all appliances and irrigation.
  2. Read the meter. Note the numbers or watch the low-flow indicator.
  3. Check the toilets. A few drops of food coloring in each tank shows a silent flapper leak if color appears in the bowl without flushing.
  4. Close the house shutoff. If the meter stops moving with the main valve closed, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is between the meter and the house.
  5. Call us with the result. That one test tells the dispatcher where to start.

Leaks in ceilings, walls and under floors

A ceiling stain usually sits below the leak, not under it. Water travels along framing before it drips. We trace the stain upward and check the nearby supply, drain and air handler condensate lines. In a wall, a leak behind a shower valve and one inside a hot water line look the same from the outside, and a moisture reading plus a pressure test tells them apart.

Pier-and-beam and raised homes have a crawlspace where leaks can run for months. Slab homes hide supply lines under the floor, and a leak there shows as a warm spot, a damp edge on carpet or the sound of running water at night. In both cases, the earlier we detect it, the less material has to come out.

Irrigation and outdoor leaks

Sprinkler zones and hose bibs leak without any sign inside the house. A broken head, a cracked valve or a wet patch that stays green in a dry week points to an irrigation leak. The Southwest Florida Water Management District sets watering restrictions for Pasco County that change with drought conditions, so a lawn that stays wet outside its watering day is worth checking.

We trace outdoor lines and tell you whether the leak is the irrigation system or the main supply.

Pool and spa lines

Pool equipment lines can leak underground as well. We find the supply side leak and refer the pool's structural or plaster leak to a pool specialist when that is the cause.

Code and standards

According to the EPA's WaterSense program, household leaks can waste nearly 10,000 gallons of water a year in an average home. That is the reason a meter test is the first step, and why a small leak is worth finding before the bill makes the case for you.

FAQ

Questions about leak detection in Zephyrhills

Do you handle leak detection emergencies at night or on weekends in Zephyrhills?

Yes. The dispatch line is open 24/7. Shut off the main valve if water is flowing and call (813) 471-5608.

How can I tell if I have a hidden leak?

Check the water meter with every fixture off. If it keeps moving, water is escaping. A sudden bill increase, damp spots, a musty smell and the sound of running water are other signs.

Will you have to cut into my walls or floor?

We try to pinpoint the leak first, so the opening is small and in the right place. Some jobs need an access hole, and we tell you before we make one.

Can you find leaks in a slab?

Yes, using listening equipment and thermal imaging. See our slab leak repair page for repair options.

Does the water utility fix a leak after the meter?

Usually the homeowner is responsible for the line from the meter to the house. Ask your water provider to confirm, and see our water line repair page.
